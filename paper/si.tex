\section{Evidence matching}
\label{si:matching}

\paragraph{Rule.} QASPER marks evidence as paragraphs of the paper's text, taken from its
\LaTeX{} source, while the app retrieves chunks of the PDF's extracted text. The two are matched
on word 5-grams. Both texts are normalised (Unicode NFKC, lower case, hyphenation at PDF line
breaks undone) and split into alphanumeric tokens. A chunk holds an evidence paragraph if it
contains at least half of the paragraph's 5-grams or, for a paragraph longer than the chunk, if
at least half of the chunk's 5-grams are in the paragraph. Evidence given only as a figure or
table is not matched. A question's evidence is \emph{findable} if any chunk of its paper holds
any of its evidence paragraphs, and \emph{retrieved} if one of the chunks passed to the model
does. The code is \texttt{matches} in \texttt{eval/qasper/run\_eval.py}.

\paragraph{Example.} For ``what language pairs are explored?'' (arXiv 1912.01214), one
annotator's evidence is the paragraph that begins ``The statistics of Europarl and MultiUN corpora
are summarized in Table TABREF18. For Europarl corpus, we evaluate on French-English-Spanish
\ldots''. The app's chunk 24 begins part-way through that paragraph (``English-French
(De-En-Fr) and Romanian-English-German \ldots'') and runs into the next one. It shares 111 of the
paragraph's 151 5-grams (74\%) and is a match. The 40 it misses come from three sources: the
opening the chunk lacks, the arrows written as \verb|$\rightarrow$| in QASPER's text and as a
symbol the tokeniser drops in the PDF's (``Fr$\rightarrow$Es''), and the citation, ``BIBREF33'' in
QASPER and ``(Sennrich, Haddow, and Birch 2015)'' in the PDF. The half-overlap threshold is there
to tolerate such differences.

\paragraph{Downloads and parsing.} Of the \BaselinePapers{} development papers,
\PapersDownloaded{} PDFs downloaded from arXiv and \PapersFailed{} returned HTTP 404; its
\PapersFailedQuestions{} questions were not asked. Every downloaded PDF gave text, so no question
was lost to parsing. Of the remaining questions, \TextEvidenceQuestions{} have text evidence.

\paragraph{Findability by chunk size.} With the app's chunker, evidence was findable for
\FindableFiveHundred{} of the \TextEvidenceQuestions{} questions with 500-character chunks,
\FindableThousand{} with 1,000 and \FindableFifteenHundred{} with 1,500 (the median question's
paper gave \MedianChunksFiveHundred{}, \MedianChunksThousand{} and \MedianChunksFifteenHundred{}
chunks). The unfindable rest were not classified; differences between the PDF text and QASPER's,
as in the example above, and between the arXiv version and the version annotated are possible
causes. Evidence is findable under all three chunk sizes for
\SweepQuestions{} questions, the set used in the retrieval sweep. The second clause of the rule
lets a short chunk match a long paragraph while holding only part of it, which favours small
chunks; this is why the leading retrieval set-ups were tested on answers
(main text, Section~4.4).

\section{Judge validation}
\label{si:judge}

\paragraph{Sample.} \JudgeCheckN{} of the 1,002 non-empty fixed-app answers, drawn with
Python's \texttt{random.Random(0).sample} from the answers sorted by question id. The judge's
last verdict for each question is used, as in the main results.

\paragraph{Labellers.} Claude (Anthropic) graded the answers first, with the judge's rules and
before reading any of its verdicts on them; those labels were committed before the comparison
was run. The author then graded the same answers blind to both the judge and Claude, on
a web page that showed the question, every annotator's reference answer and the app's answer,
with three buttons and an optional note. Neither labeller read the papers.

\paragraph{Instructions shown to the human labeller.} Judge the app's answer against the
reference answers; one matching reference is enough. Correct: gives the reference's content;
extra detail is fine if it does not contradict it. Partially correct: part of the reference, or
the right content mixed with something wrong. Incorrect: wrong, or misses the point. An answer
saying the paper does not give this is correct only when an annotator marked the question
unanswerable. Grade the content only, not the page citations. Three questions from the sample
(1, 3 and 5 below) were explained as examples before labelling.

\paragraph{Rule check.} In four answers (marked $\dagger$) the human label contradicts the
instructions where they leave no room for judgement: an annotator's reference matches the answer.
Counting them as correct is the exploratory sensitivity check reported in the main text; it was
made after the judge's verdicts had been seen.

\section{The app in a browser}
\label{si:ui}

Llama~3.1~8B in Ollama (the weights behind Groq's \texttt{llama-3.1-8b-instant}) and every request
logged. A script uploaded the PDFs, typed the questions and took the screenshots. The full
screenshots, the request logs and the scripts are in \texttt{eval/results/2026-11-01-ui-check}
and \texttt{2026-11-01-ui-check-multidoc}; the crops below are made by
